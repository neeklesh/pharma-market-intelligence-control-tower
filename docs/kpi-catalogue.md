# KPI Catalogue

## 1. Purpose
Define the KPIs and analytical indicators required to support market intelligence, competitive intelligence, clinical pipeline, regulatory intelligence, pricing, commercial analytics and forecasting.

This catalogue represents initial project definitions. KPI methodologies, data sources and weights will be validated as the project develops.

### KPI-MI-001: Country Market Attractiveness Score

Business Purpose: Support strategic comparison and prioritisation of selected APAC markets.

Primary Users:
- Regional Strategy Manager
- Marketing Head
- Regional Leadership

Proposed Components:

| Component | Proposed Weight |
|---|---:|
| Disease Burden | 25% |
| Population Opportunity | 20% |
| Healthcare Access | 15% |
| Economic Attractiveness | 15% |
| Market Development | 15% |
| Competitive Environment | 10% |
| Total | 100% |

Proposed formula:

Market Attractiveness Score = Sum of (Component Weight × Standardised Component Score)

Each component will be standardised to an appropriate scale, initially proposed as 0–100.

Assumptions and limitations:
- Weights are provisional project assumptions.
- Indicator directionality must be documented.
- Missing data must be handled transparently.
- Country comparisons must consider differences in data quality and availability.
- Competitive intensity may have positive or negative implications depending on context.
- The score is a decision-support indicator, not an investment recommendation.

## 9. KPI Governance

Each KPI should eventually have:

- KPI ID
- KPI Name
- Business Definition
- Business Purpose
- Formula
- Unit of Measure
- Reporting Frequency
- Data Source
- Dimensions
- Business Owner
- Data Owner
- Data Quality Considerations
- Limitations
- Validation Method

## 10. Important Data and Methodology Principles

- Document all assumptions.
- Use source traceability.
- Clearly identify synthetic data.
- Avoid presenting estimates as actual commercial market sales.
- Document standardisation methods.
- Document missing-data handling.
- Validate calculations.
- Distinguish descriptive metrics from KPIs.
- Avoid interpreting composite scores without understanding their components.
